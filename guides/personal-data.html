Keeping personal data safe

Protect your own details, and handle customers' and staff data the right way.

“Personal data” is any information about a living person — names, addresses, emails, phone numbers, dates of birth, card details, photos, health or anything that identifies someone. Keeping it safe protects you from fraud and identity theft, and if you run a business, it's also the law (UK GDPR). Here's the simple version.

Protect your own personal data

  • Use strong, separate passwords and 2-factor login on email and banking — email is the key to resetting everything else. See passwords & 2FA.
  • Share less. Think before posting birthdays, pet names, your address or holiday dates on social media — scammers use these to guess passwords and answers.
  • Tighten your privacy settings on social media and your phone so strangers can't see everything.
  • Check if you've been in a breach. A free site like haveibeenpwned.com tells you if your email has appeared in a known leak — change those passwords.
  • Be stingy with your details. If a form or app doesn't need your date of birth or full address, don't give it.

If you hold other people's data (customers, staff)

Running a business means you're responsible for the personal data you hold. The principles are common sense:

  • Collect only what you need, and keep it only as long as you need it. Less data = less to lose.
  • Limit who can see it. Staff get access to what their job needs, no more. Remove access when people leave.
  • Protect it — strong accounts with 2FA, encrypted laptops/phones, and good backups.
  • Share it safely — don't email lists of personal data unprotected; use a secure/encrypted method, and double-check the address before sending.
  • Keep it accurate, and be ready to show someone what you hold about them if they ask.

Get rid of it safely

  • Wipe devices (factory reset with encryption on) before you sell, recycle or bin a phone, laptop or old hard drive.
  • Shred paper with names, addresses or card details — don't just bin it.
  • Empty old accounts. Close online accounts you no longer use, so your data isn't sitting in them.
UK business basics: most businesses that handle personal data must pay a small annual data-protection fee to the ICO (ico.org.uk) and follow UK GDPR. If personal data is lost or exposed in a way that could harm people, you may need to report it to the ICO within 72 hours. The ICO website has plain-English guides for small businesses.

This is general awareness, not legal advice — for your specific duties, check the ICO's small-business guidance or get professional advice.
