Phones & tablets

Your phone holds email, banking and your login codes — keep it locked down.

Your phone or tablet is a pocket-sized computer that holds your email, often your banking, your photos and the login codes that protect everything else. If someone gets into it, they can get into a lot — so a few simple habits really matter.

Lock it down

  • Use a 6-digit PIN or biometrics (fingerprint/face), not a 4-digit code or a swipe pattern.
  • Set it to lock quickly — auto-lock after 30 seconds to a minute.
  • Encryption is on by default on modern iPhones and Androids — just keep a screen lock set, and it protects your data if the phone is lost.
  • Hide message previews on the lock screen, so login codes and texts aren't readable without unlocking.

Keep it updated

  • Turn on automatic updates for the operating system (iOS / Android) and your apps.
  • Replace phones that stop getting updates. An old phone with no more security updates is a weak point.

Apps

  • Only install from the official App Store or Google Play.
  • Check what an app can access (contacts, location, microphone, camera). If a torch app wants your contacts, say no.
  • Delete apps you don't use — fewer apps, less risk.

Scam texts and calls

A lot of attacks arrive by text (“smishing”) or phone call — fake delivery notices, “your bank”, “HMRC refund”. The same rules as phishing apply: don't tap links, don't give codes or passwords, and check with the company on a number you already have. Forward scam texts to 7726.

Watch for SIM swap

If your phone suddenly loses all signal for no reason, it can be a sign someone has hijacked your number to steal your login codes. Contact your mobile provider straight away, and ask them to put a PIN/passcode on your mobile account so no one can move your number without it.

Lost or stolen

  • Turn on Find My iPhone / Find My Device now, so you can lock or wipe it remotely later.
  • If it's lost: lock it remotely, then wipe it if you can't get it back.
  • Tell whoever manages your IT, and change important passwords from another device.
For the business: if staff use their own phones for work email, ask them to set a screen lock and keep the phone updated — and make sure you can remove work email from a lost phone (your email system can do this).
