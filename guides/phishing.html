Spotting phishing emails & texts

The scam emails and texts that trick staff into clicking, paying or sharing passwords.

Phishing is a fake message — email, text or even a phone call — pretending to be someone you trust, to make you click a bad link, open an attachment, hand over a password, or pay money. It's the most common way small businesses get hit, because it only takes one busy person clicking once.

The warning signs — stop if you see these

  • It rushes or scares you. “Act now”, “your account will be closed”, “final demand”. Pressure is the number-one trick.
  • It asks for a password, PIN or card details. Real companies never ask for these by email or text.
  • The sender looks slightly off. A familiar name but a strange address behind it (hover or tap to reveal it).
  • The link doesn't match. Hover over it — if the real address is different or odd, don't click.
  • Unexpected attachment. Especially .zip or .html, or one that asks you to “enable content”.
  • Odd greeting or spelling. “Dear Customer”, wrong company name, clumsy grammar.
  • Too good to be true. Refunds, prizes, parcels you didn't order.

What to do with a suspicious message

  1. Stop — don't click links or open attachments.
  2. Check the sender — hover over or tap the name to see the real email address.
  3. Verify another way — if it claims to be a colleague, supplier or your bank, contact them on a number you already have, not one in the message.
  4. Report it (see below), then delete it.
Report it (UK, free):
  • Suspicious emails → forward to report@phishing.gov.uk (NCSC Suspicious Email Reporting Service).
  • Suspicious texts → forward to 7726 (spells “SPAM” on the keypad).
  • Lost money or data → Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040 (in Scotland, call Police on 101).

For the business owner

  • Tell staff these messages happen, and that reporting is never a bother — a fast report limits the damage.
  • Turn on 2-factor login so a stolen password alone isn't enough.
  • Consider occasional friendly test emails to see who needs a refresher (train, don't blame).
