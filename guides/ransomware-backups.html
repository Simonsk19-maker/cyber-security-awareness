Ransomware & backups

How businesses get locked out of their files, and the backups that get you back.

Ransomware is nasty software that locks or scrambles your files and demands a payment to unlock them. For a small business it can mean losing access to everything — orders, accounts, customer records — overnight. Good backups are what get you back without paying.

How it gets in

  • A clicked link or attachment in a phishing email.
  • An out-of-date device missing security updates.
  • Weak or reused passwords on remote access, with no 2-factor login.

Back up the 3-2-1 way

  • 3 copies of anything important,
  • 2 different places or types of storage,
  • 1 copy kept off-site and disconnected (e.g. a reputable cloud backup, or a drive that isn't always plugged in).

The disconnected copy matters: ransomware encrypts everything it can reach, including backup drives left plugged in. Cloud backup with version history means you can roll back to before the attack.

Do test a restore now and then — a backup you've never restored from is a guess, not a safety net.

Lower your risk

  • Keep devices and software updated.
  • Use antivirus and leave Windows Defender / your security software switched on.
  • Turn on 2-factor login everywhere, especially remote access.
  • Give staff only the access they need (not everyone needs to be an administrator).
Hit by ransomware? Don't pay if you can avoid it (there's no guarantee you get your files back). Disconnect the device from the network, and see If something goes wrong.
