Passwords & 2-factor login

Strong passphrases, password managers, and the one setting that stops most break-ins.

Weak or reused passwords are how a lot of break-ins happen. The good news: a few simple habits — and one free setting called 2-factor login — stop the vast majority.

Make strong passwords the easy way

  • Use three random words for important passwords, e.g. coffee-rocket-plum. Long and memorable beats short and complicated.
  • Never reuse the password for email or banking anywhere else — email is the master key to everything.
  • Change default passwords on anything new (routers, cameras, Wi-Fi, admin accounts).
  • Don't share passwords by email or sticky note.

Use a password manager

A password manager remembers a different strong password for every account, so your team only needs to remember one. It also warns you if a site you use has been breached. It's the single biggest time-saver for password safety.

Turn on 2-factor login (2FA / MFA)

2-factor login asks for a second thing as well as your password — usually a code from an app on your phone. So even if a criminal steals the password, they still can't get in.

Do turn it on first for: email, Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace, banking, your website, and anything with customer data.
Avoid where you can: text-message codes are better than nothing, but an authenticator app (or a security key) is safer.
Owner tip: make 2-factor login required for all staff on your email and 365/Google admin — it's the most effective single control for a small business, and it's free.
