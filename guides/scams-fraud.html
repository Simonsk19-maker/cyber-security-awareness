Invoice & impersonation fraud

Fake invoices, 'the boss' asking for an urgent payment, and bogus suppliers.

Not all attacks involve dodgy software. Some of the costliest frauds are simply convincing messages aimed at whoever handles money or suppliers.

The big ones to know

ScamWhat it looks likeThe giveaway
Invoice / mandate fraudA real supplier “emails” to say their bank details have changed — pay the next invoice to a new account.Changed bank details. Always verify by phoning the supplier on a known number.
CEO / boss fraud“The MD” urgently needs a payment or gift cards, and asks you to keep it quiet.Urgency + secrecy + money. Check in person or by phone.
Fake supplier / overpaymentA new “customer” overpays and asks for the difference back; or a fake supplier wants payment up front.Pressure to refund or pay fast. Slow down and verify.
Phone scams (vishing)“Your bank” or “Microsoft” calls about a problem and asks for remote access or codes.Nobody legitimate asks for passwords, codes or remote access out of the blue. Hang up and call back on a known number.

Simple rules that stop most of it

  • Verify any change of bank details by phone, using a number you already have — never the one in the email.
  • Two people for big payments. A second check for anything over a set amount.
  • Urgency + secrecy + money = stop. That combination is the fraudster's fingerprint.
  • Never give codes, passwords or remote access to someone who called or messaged you.
If you've paid a fraudster: contact your bank immediately (many can try to stop or recall the payment), then report to Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040 (Scotland: Police 101).
