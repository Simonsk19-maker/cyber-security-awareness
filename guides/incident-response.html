If something goes wrong

Calm, step-by-step actions — and who to report it to in the UK.

If you think you've been hacked, scammed or infected, don't panic — quick, calm steps limit the damage. Keep this page handy.

First steps

  1. Disconnect, don't destroy. Take the affected device off Wi-Fi / unplug the network cable, but leave it switched on if you can — it helps work out what happened.
  2. Change passwords from a different, clean device — email first, then banking and anything important. Turn on 2-factor login while you're there.
  3. Tell your bank immediately if any money or card details are involved — they may be able to stop or recall a payment.
  4. Tell your IT support. The sooner someone competent looks, the better.

Then

  • Check your backups before wiping anything (see Ransomware & backups).
  • Warn your team — if one person was phished, others may have had the same message.
  • Keep a note of what happened and when; it helps reporting and prevention.
Report it (UK):
  • Action Fraud (fraud & cyber crime, England/Wales/NI): 0300 123 2040 or actionfraud.police.uk. In Scotland, call Police on 101.
  • Scam emails → report@phishing.gov.uk; scam texts → 7726.
  • A personal-data breach may need reporting to the ICO within 72 hours (ico.org.uk) — check if customer data was exposed.

Afterwards — stop it happening again

  • Fix the way in (patch the device, reset the account, add 2-factor login).
  • Do a quick team refresher using these guides.
  • Make sure your backups actually work.
